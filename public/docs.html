Step 1, coach instructions

Write the coach

In claude.ai, create a new Project and name it something like "Sam's PT". Paste the template below into its instructions and fill every bracket. Vague inputs get vague programming.

IdentityHow it talks. "Direct, evidence-based" beats a paragraph.
Athlete profileExperience, goal, days per week, kit, anything that limits training.
Working weightsWhat you actually lift. Say they're not one-rep maxes.
Programming rulesRPE targets, when to add weight, by how much, deloads.

Project instructions template

IDENTITY
You are a personal trainer AI built for [name]. Direct, knowledgeable,
evidence-based. Talk like a coach who actually trains.

ATHLETE PROFILE
Training experience: [x years]
Goal: [e.g. gain lean muscle, lose fat]
Schedule: [days a week, which ones are cardio]
Equipment: [home kit and heaviest dumbbell; gym]
Limitations: [injuries, conditions that affect training]

CURRENT WORKING WEIGHTS
These are working weights, NOT one-rep maxes. Never calculate
percentages from them. For an exercise not listed, check the lift log.
If it isn't there, estimate, flag it as a calibration weight, and
confirm the real weight after the first session.
[Exercise]: [kg]
[Exercise]: [kg]

LIFT LOG
Append each session in this format:
[DATE] Session name
Exercise | Sets x Reps | Weight | RPE | Notes
Session notes: [energy, how it felt]
Never delete old entries. After a few sessions, the log replaces the
working weights above as the main reference.

PROGRAMMING RULES
- RPE 7 to 8 for hypertrophy, 8 to 9 for strength
- Add weight after hitting the top of the rep range at target RPE
  for 2 sessions in a row: upper compounds +2.5 kg, lower +5 kg,
  accessories add reps before weight
- Suggest a deload every 4 to 6 weeks, or when I flag fatigue
- Week 1 of any new programme is a calibration week
- Base each session on the most recent log entries

HOW TO RESPOND
- When I log a session: acknowledge it, update the log, note any
  weight increases, flag anything to adjust
- When I ask for the next session: a table with weights, sets,
  reps and RPE
- When I ask about progress: concrete numbers from the log
- Keep it short. Tables for sessions. No waffle.
- If you can't edit my log file, give me the updated file named
  YYYY-MM-DD Session name so I can save it myself
Paste it into the Project's instructions

Want a personality on top, like bluntness or jokes? Put that in your Claude profile preferences instead, so it applies everywhere and the Project stays about training.

Step 2, lift log

Start the log

Keep the log as one markdown file you own, outside Claude. A notes app like Obsidian works well, since it's plain text and syncs to your phone. Every session gets appended, never rewritten.

One entry, 5 Oct, Session A

ExerciseSetskgRPENotes
Bench press3 × 8308Last set grindy
Lat pulldown3 × 10367Easy, add weight
Leg press3 × 121108Total, not per side
NotesSlept 6 hours, energy 6 out of 10. Shoulder fine.

The update loop

  1. Upload the current log to the Project, or attach it to the chat.
  2. Send the session: raw notes, a screenshot, or "pull today's workout".
  3. Claude parses it, flags anything odd, and returns the updated file.
  4. Replace your copy with that file and re-upload it to the Project.

The Project's copy doesn't update itself. Skipping step 4 is how the coach ends up programming off last week.

Step 3, files and knowledge

Give it context

Instructions say how to coach. Project knowledge is what the coach checks before it answers. A Project holds two kinds of knowledge, and it helps to know which is which.

FilesThings you upload as they are: PDF reports, screenshots, photos. Reference material that doesn't change once it's in.
DocsText documents you keep up to date: the lift log, your programme, your exercise library, your learnings.

Files worth uploading

FileWhat the coach uses it forRefresh
DEXA scanFat mass, lean mass by limb, visceral fat. The baseline every cut or bulk is judged against.8 to 12 weeks
Body comp scanQuick checks between DEXAs. Only comparable if taken the same way: morning, fasted, same machine.4 weeks
Blood testsThyroid, iron, vitamin D, lipids. Explains low energy before the programme gets blamed.3 to 6 months
Progress photosFront, side, back, same light. Shows what the scale misses.4 weeks
ScreenshotsA nutrition diary, a watch summary, a machine's weight plate. Faster than typing.As needed

Make files easy to use

  • Put the date and type in the name: "2026-09-04 DEXA.pdf", not "scan_final2.pdf".
  • Upload reports as text PDFs where you can. A photo of a printout reads worse.
  • Keep old scans. The coach needs them to show a trend, so add the new one beside the old.
  • When you upload a new file, say so in chat: "new DEXA is in, compare it to April".
  • Delete files that are wrong, not files that are old. Two conflicting truths make bad advice.

Lines from a real learnings file

  • Iso-lateral machines: the carriage has its own weight. Log the real load per side.
  • Assisted pull-up: more counterweight means easier, not harder.
  • Zone 2 is my heart-rate band, 130 to 140 bpm, not my wearable's zone label.
  • Never superset chest with triceps. They tire each other out.
  • Don't raise any weights after a night under six hours of sleep.

Turn memory on for the Project too. It picks up your preferences between chats, but the files stay the authority.

When a Project holds a lot, Claude searches its knowledge rather than reading everything at once. Clear titles and headings are how it finds the right page.

Step 4, data sources

Connect your data

Typing every set into a chat gets old fast. Pull what you can, screenshot the rest. Where a service has a connector in Claude, add it under Settings, Connectors.

GarminMain record for lifting and cardio sessions. When two sources disagree, it wins.
StravaHas a Claude connector. Gives duration, heart rate and calories, but not sets and reps. The activity description from Garmin carries the lifts.
WhoopSleep and recovery. Its Strava copies are duplicates: spot them by "Strain" in the description.
MyFitnessPalProtein and calories. A diary screenshot is the most reliable way in.

A good pull pattern: list activities for the date, then fetch that one activity's details. Ask for both in one message.

Every gym day

The loop

Plan"Heading to the gym." It reads the log and your sleep, then proposes a table.
ApprovePush back on anything. Nothing is final until you say yes.
TrainLog weight, reps and RPE as you go, in notes or your watch.
ReviewSend the session. It updates the log and working weights, and flags issues.

Then the review feeds the next plan. Every 4 to 6 weeks, a deload.

Optional, for builders

Level up: your own app

Chat works, but copying plans in and logs out is the weak spot. Olympus fixes that with a workout app and an MCP connector, so Claude reads and writes your data directly.

What the connector does

  • Reads your profile, recent sessions and recovery before every plan.
  • Pushes the approved plan straight to the app's Today screen.
  • Checks every plan against rules first, like blocked exercises or a weight that jumps too far.
  • Has no delete tools. Anything removed, you remove in the app.

Mistakes to skip

Each of these happened once, then became a rule.

Opening setsCalibration weights run lightHitting 15 reps on the first set means the guess was too low. Ask for exact opening loads, not general advice.
MachinesRead the machine, not the stackCarriage weights and counterweights flip the maths. Write them into your learnings file on day one.
SupersetsBan pairings in writingIf a pairing tires you out, say so in the instructions. A rule in chat is forgotten next week.
ProgressionCap the jumpsA machine squat once went from 50 to 90 kg in one plan. Set a maximum increase per session.
NutritionTurn off exercise caloriesCalorie apps add back exercise by default. That quietly erases a deficit.
Your callYou can drop a liftIf your form on an exercise worries you, cut it permanently and log the decision so it stays cut.
